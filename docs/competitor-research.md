# Free Agent HQ: Competitive Landscape and Business Model

*Research sweep dated 2026-11-03. Sources are web search results. Direct page fetches were mostly blocked in this session (opendorse.com and netinfluencer.com failed to resolve), so most facts come from search-result summaries of the cited URLs.*

**Confidence key:** **[V]** means two or more consistent or primary sources agree. **[S]** means a single or secondary source. **[UNVERIFIED]** means the sources conflict, are low quality, or are missing.

---

## 0. Executive summary

- **The market is split up.** Nobody serves the whole athlete career (opportunities, deals, pricing, contract checks, evaluation) across all sports and levels. Each player covers one slice:
  - NIL marketplaces sell deal flow, mostly to college athletes.
  - Recruiting services sell exposure to high-school families.
  - Pro scouting tools sell data to teams.
  - Traditional agents serve the top roughly 5% of athletes.
- **Money is moving.** Revenue sharing began on July 1, 2025. Opendorse projects collective-funded NIL will fall from more than $1.3B (2024-25) to $227M (2025-26) and $78M by 2028-29. NIL marketplaces built around collectives are losing their main buyer. Their pivots are to brands, retail-media and fan subscriptions, plus picking up failed operators (for example, Opendorse took on clients of SANIL).
- **Compliance is now a gate.** Third-party college deals of $600 or more must clear the NIL Go portal (Deloitte, for the College Sports Commission). Through June 2026, NIL Go cleared about $355M and rejected about $90M. Deals fail on "valid business purpose" or "range of compensation." No athlete-side tool predicts whether a deal will clear before it is signed.
- **The biggest open space is the post-college pro pathway:** undrafted free agents (UDFAs), the UFL, CFL, overseas leagues and minor leagues. Today this runs on league-run, pay-to-attend showcases (UFL: $265 or $395) and scam-prone overseas exposure camps. No AI tool matches players to tryouts, and none scores the legitimacy of an opportunity.
- **"AI agent" products are early.** MOGL launched a free AI Athlete Assistant (2026). JABA AI signed the University of Cincinnati (Feb 2026). Tykoon AI targets HBCU athletes. All of them are about NIL content and deal admin. None covers pro opportunities, contracts or scouting.
- **Regulatory design constraint.** Most state athlete-agent acts (UAAA/RUAAA) define "athlete agent" as an *individual* who recruits, solicits or negotiates for an athlete. A self-serve software tool is probably outside that definition. But it should never solicit athletes for representation, never negotiate as the athlete's agent, and never take a contingent cut that looks like an agency fee. The FTC opened a SPARTA (federal Sports Agent Responsibility and Trust Act) inquiry in Jan 2026. Union fee caps (NFLPA 3%, NBPA 4%) only apply to certified agents.

## 1. NIL and athlete-brand marketplaces

### Opendorse
- **What it offers:** NIL marketplace (Opendorse Deals), a tool for collectives and schools to run payments, deal disclosure and compliance, fan subscriptions ("Opendorse Clubs"), and a retail-media product tying athlete content to purchases. It also publishes an annual NIL market report.
- **Who pays:** Schools, collectives and brands pay through an enterprise subscription plus a fee on each transaction. Athletes pay $0. **[S]** canvasbusinessmodel.com, nilclub.com comparison.
- **Pricing:** Competitor blogs cite a **30% transaction fee** charged to the buyer. **[UNVERIFIED: competitor sources only]** (launchpointhq.com/blog/opendorse-reviews-pricing-alternatives; nilclub.com/blog/opendorse-vs-nil-club...). An older breakdown lists a "50% to NIL rights royalties" split on some payment types. **[UNVERIFIED, likely outdated]**
- **Funding and scale:**
  - About $40M total raised after a $20M round (2023). Front Office Sports and SportBusiness (frontofficesports.com/article/nil-company-opendorse-raises-20m-in-funding/; sportbusiness.com/news/opendorse-raises-20m-in-latest-funding-round/). **[V]**
  - CB Insights totals range from $34M to $53M. **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - Laid off about 20-23% of staff in Nov 2024, leaving about 50 employees (sportico.com/leagues/college-sports/2024/opendorse-layoffs-nil-tech-industry-1234816704/). **[V]**
  - Took over clients of SANIL, a failed collective operator (frontofficesports.com/article/opendorse-is-taking-over-parts-of-failed-nil-collective-operator-sanil/).
  - Says collectives sent about $20M to athletes through Opendorse on June 30, 2025.
  - Put the NIL market at more than $4B (footballscoop.com, June 2026).

- **Strengths:** Brand recognition, school and collective integrations, data and reports, payment rails.
- **Gaps and complaints:** Built around collectives, a revenue base it projects will shrink about 80% or more. Heavy take rate. College only. No pro-pathway or scouting features. The marketplace has to wait for brands to find athletes.

- **Who pays:** Schools and pro teams (SaaS).

- **Strengths:** Embedded with schools. The school is the system of record for compliance and revenue sharing. Strong data and AI budget.

## 6. Regulatory context and product design implications

### House v. NCAA settlement (final approval June 2025)
- Schools may share revenue directly with athletes. Cap: $20.5M per school in 2025-26 **[V]** and about $21.3M in 2026-27 **[S: explainer sites]**, growing about 4%/yr.
- 68 schools hit or nearly hit the cap in year one (lanacion.com.ar/AP).
- Roster limits replace scholarship limits (football: 105).
- The College Sports Commission (CSC) enforces.

### College Sports Commission / NIL Go
- D-I athletes must report third-party deals of $600 or more in aggregate. Payors have no portal access (floridagators.com/sports/2025/6/10/nil-go).
- **Three review steps** (burr.com):
  1. Is the payor associated with the school (booster or collective)?
  2. Does the deal have a valid business purpose, meaning selling goods or services to the public for profit?
  3. Is the pay within the range of compensation for similarly situated athletes?
- **Results:**
  - Through Jan 2026: 17,321 deals cleared ($127M) and 524 rejected ($14.9M) (forbes.com/sites/kristidosh/2026/01/13/...).
  - Through June 30, 2026: about $355M cleared and about $90M rejected (espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/49308194/nil-clearinghouse-rejected-90m-deals).
  - May-June 2026: 7,639 cleared ($112.9M) and 659 denied ($33.7M) (on3.com).
  - About 20% of submitted dollars were not cleared (Duane Morris).
  - An arbitrator upheld denial of $7.5M in Playfly/Nebraska deals. Another arbitrator ruled for Georgia athletes.
- **Design implication:** A "will this clear?" pre-check is valuable. It needs comparable-deal pricing, a payor business-purpose check, and auto-generated NIL Go disclosure packets. Athletes file, so the athlete-side tool is the right place for this.

### State athlete-agent laws (UAAA 2000 / RUAAA 2015)
- Adopted in some form by about 40+ states. California uses Miller-Ayala instead.
- RUAAA defines "athlete agent" as an **individual** who enters an agency contract, or who recruits or solicits an athlete to enter one, or (for some) who represents an athlete for pay. It requires registration, notice to the school, contract warnings and a cancellation right. Drafting notes say only individuals are covered (ulc.wa.gov RUAAA PDF; NRS 398A; Mississippi SOS 2025 act).
- North Carolina (2025) added NIL-specific rules: a conspicuous-notice requirement, and NIL agency contracts are void if the athlete then enrolls at that institution (ncleg.gov ch. 78C art. 9).
- **No statute or case was found deciding whether a software platform is an "athlete agent." [UNVERIFIED / open question; get counsel]**
- **Risk factors** that raise the chance of being treated as an agent:
  - Humans on staff who solicit athletes or negotiate on their behalf.
  - Contingent fees taken from the athlete's compensation.
  - Exclusive representation clauses.
  - Contacting teams or brands *as the athlete's representative*.
- **Lower-risk design:**
  - The athlete stays the negotiating party, and the AI drafts while the athlete sends.
  - Subscription or flat fees, with no percentage of athlete compensation.
  - Fees paid by brands and teams for marketplace access, structured as a software fee rather than a cut of the athlete's pay.
  - A licensed-agent or attorney referral network for anything beyond self-serve.
  - Federal SPARTA bans misleading information and inducements to student-athletes, and the FTC sent inquiries to 20 schools in Jan 2026 (wiley.law). Keep marketing to high-school and college athletes truthful.
  - Pending: Protect College Sports Act (Senate, 2026) **[UNVERIFIED status]** and Florida HB 981 fee-cap bill **[UNVERIFIED passage]** (sportico.com/leagues/college-sports/2025/florida-bill-athlete-agent-fees-cap-1234832154/).

### Union agent rules
- NFLPA/NBPA certification is required for anyone who *negotiates player contracts* with clubs. The fee caps (3% and 4%) apply to certified agents.
- A self-represented player may use software. Free Agent HQ must not negotiate with NFL or NBA clubs on a player's behalf unless it is a certified agent.
- Recommendation: a "Certified Agent Marketplace" where certified agents bid, with Free Agent HQ charging the agent a SaaS or lead fee. Check NFLPA rules on fee-splitting and referral payments before building this. **[UNVERIFIED: need to read current NFLPA Regulations]**

## 7. (a) The biggest gaps across the market

1. **No pro-pathway engine.** No product gathers NFL rookie-camp and minicamp tryouts, UFL, CFL, European League of Football and other spring and overseas leagues, minor-league baseball and hockey, G League, and international basketball and soccer openings. None matches them to a player's profile. Today the options are league-run paid showcases and word of mouth.
2. **No legitimacy scoring.** Overseas camps and "pay to get signed" offers are a known scam category. Nobody verifies opportunities, camps or agents.
3. **Athletes lose their tools when eligibility ends.** Teamworks, INFLCR and Athliance are licensed by schools and stop when the athlete leaves, at the moment of greatest need.
4. **No pricing for an individual athlete's deal.** On3 valuations are disputed projections. SponsorUnited prices properties. Athletes don't know what a deal should pay.
5. **No pre-check for NIL Go clearance.** About 20% of submitted NIL dollars were not cleared. Athletes find out after signing.
6. **No affordable contract review.** The choice is a 10-30% agent or an hourly lawyer. Nothing offers AI clause checks against a typical-terms database (exclusivity, morals clauses, perpetual-rights grabs, agent-term traps).
7. **Scouting tools serve teams, not athletes.** Hudl, Wyscout, Zelus and SkillCorner are expensive and team-licensed. Athletes never see an objective report on their own strengths, weaknesses and scheme fit that they can send to scouts.
8. **Discovery is broken on the team side for long-tail talent.** GMs and scouts at the UFL, CFL, overseas and D-II/NAIA levels have no searchable, verified database of available free agents with standardized stats, film and testing data. aiScout proves demand, but only in soccer.
9. **Film can't be trusted.** AI-doctored highlights are rising, and coaches want authenticated film and data.
10. **Single-sport, single-level silos.** NIL players are college only. NCSA is high school only. Wyscout is soccer pros. No product follows an athlete from high school to college to pro to post-career across all sports.
11. **Business models extract from athletes or hide prices.** Opendorse's 30% buyer fee **[UNVERIFIED]**, NCSA's opaque $1K-4K packages, non-refundable showcase fees, and 20-30% NIL agent cuts.
12. **Collective-dependent marketplaces are shrinking.** Their deal flow is falling and they are pivoting to brands and fans. Small and local brands remain underserved. They need self-serve, compliant, priced deals.

## 8. (b) Proposed business model for Free Agent HQ

**Core principle:** *Athletes never pay a percentage. Discovery is free for athletes. Paying customers are the people who want access to athletes: teams, scouts, brands and leagues.* This keeps the athlete side growing fast, avoids looking like an "agent," and undercuts every agent and pay-to-play model.

### Revenue streams

| # | Stream | Who pays | Pricing (suggested) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Athlete subscription (freemium) | Athlete or family | **Free:** profile, verified stats import, 3 opportunity matches/mo, 1 AI scouting report, basic NIL Go pre-check. **Pro $19/mo or $149/yr:** unlimited matches, full AI scouting reports, deal pricing, 5 contract reviews/mo, negotiation copilot, verified film badge. **Pro Pathway $49/mo, or $299 per "season" (Jan-Aug draft and tryout cycle):** tryout alerts, application autopilot, scout outreach CRM, workout and testing verification, overseas legitimacy checks. |
| 2 | Contract review à la carte | Athlete | **$29** AI review. **$149** AI plus partner-attorney review (Free Agent HQ takes 20-30% of the attorney fee as a platform fee; check state referral-fee and ethics rules). |
| 3 | Team, scout and GM seats ("Scout Desk") | Pro and semi-pro teams, college programs, overseas clubs | **Starter $99/mo** (D-II/NAIA, juco, small overseas clubs). **Pro $499/mo per seat** (UFL, CFL, ELF, G League, USL). **Enterprise $25K-150K/yr** (NFL, NBA, MLB orgs, top college programs, league-wide licenses with API). |
| 4 | Virtual trials and verified combines (aiScout model) | Leagues and teams | **$5K-50K per campaign.** Teams post a trial. Athletes apply free with phone-verified testing. Partner with UFL and CFL to replace or feed their $265-395 showcases. |
| 5 | NIL and brand marketplace | Brands, local businesses | **Transparent 10% brand-side platform fee** (vs. 20-30% at competitors; 30% Opendorse figure is unverified). Self-serve brand plans **$0 / $99 / $499 per month.** Fee is never deducted from athlete pay. Includes NIL Go pre-clearance and disclosure packet. |
| 6 | Data and pricing intelligence | Brands, agencies, schools, collectives | Anonymized deal comparables and "range of compensation" API. **$10K-100K/yr.** Benchmarks against SponsorUnited's SPND for individual athletes. |
| 7 | School and conference license (optional, later) | Athletic departments | **$5K-40K/yr** for a white-label athlete career hub. Athletes keep their account after leaving school, which is the key difference from Teamworks. |
| 8 | Certified-agent and advisor marketplace | Agents, financial advisors, trainers | Lead or subscription fee (**$199-999/mo**) for vetted pros to receive opt-in requests from athletes. The athlete chooses, and Free Agent HQ never represents. Must be checked against NFLPA/NBPA referral rules. |

### Why this beats competitors

| Competitor type | What athletes pay there | What athletes pay at Free Agent HQ |
|---|---|---|
| Agents | 3-4% on playing contracts plus 10-20% on endorsements (20-30% from NIL agents) | $0-$49/mo flat |
| NCSA | Hundreds to thousands of dollars | Pro tier at $149/yr |
| UFL showcase | $265-395, non-refundable | Free athlete applications to virtual trials, which teams fund |
| NIL marketplaces | Buyer fee of up to about 30% | About 10% brand-side fee |
| Teamworks / INFLCR | Access ends with eligibility | Athlete owns the account for life |

### Network effects and moats
1. **Two-sided marketplace.** More verified athletes attract more scouts and brands, which brings more opportunities and in turn more athletes. Free discovery for athletes seeds supply.
2. **Proprietary outcome data.** Every tryout result, signing, contract term and NIL deal outcome (cleared or rejected) trains the pricing, NIL Go pre-check and fit models. Competitors see only their own slice. This becomes the "range of compensation" dataset that even schools and brands will license.
3. **Verification layer.** Phone-verified testing, coach-endorsed stats (FieldLevel-style trust) and authenticated film become the trust standard scouts rely on. This is hard to copy once teams depend on it.
4. **Lifetime athlete identity.** One profile from high school to college to pro to post-career, across all sports. The switching cost grows with history.
5. **Opportunity graph.** A curated, legitimacy-scored database of tryouts, leagues, clubs and agents, kept current by crowd reports plus staff review.
6. **Compliance by design.** Structured as a tool, not an agent. This avoids registration in about 40 states and the union fee caps, so Free Agent HQ can price below every agent.
